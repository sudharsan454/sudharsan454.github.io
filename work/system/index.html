How I work

The system behind the work

I solve problems. AI is one of the ways. Every piece of work on this site went through the same four steps, and I turned each step into a reusable skill, so the loop runs faster and my team can run it without me.

1. Find the problem worth solving

Discovery Impact Triage. Turns call notes into one clearly stated problem. For a customer already live, it checks what people said against their real records and settings first.

2. Build the fix

Agent and workflow builder. Takes a customer's workflow and produces the rules, the prompt, every scenario the agent must handle, and the test run. When the fix isn't AI, it stops at the workflow.

3. Prove it works

Eval builder and verdict. Scores each test round against the actual records in the system, not the reply text. One answer at the end: ready for customers or not, and why.

4. Keep it working

Report checker. Checks each customer performance report against the raw data before it goes out. The numbers can be right while the report is still wrong.

When AI fits

Choosing AI is a decision I make per problem, not a default. A use case has to pass four checks. Most don't, and that's fine.

  • Repeats every day. High volume, same shape. A one-off doesn't pay for a model.
  • Can be automated. The steps are known and a system can act on them, not just suggest.
  • Messy input. Calls, emails, PDFs, photos. Unstructured data is where AI earns its place over rules.
  • Worth it to both sides. The user gets time back and the business gets something it can price and measure.

And humans keep what they should. I design the handoff to people on day one, at the boundary the customer draws. AI does the repeat work; people make the calls that need judgement.

What the first step looks like

A real discovery output for a hospital FM prospect. Every problem we heard is grouped, given an owner, and either taken into the pilot or deliberately held back, with the reason in plain words. The top pick was the problem two people raised on their own, in two different situations, with the same root cause.

How I run my own week

PMOS is the operating system I built for myself. Every request lands as a task, every call I make is logged as a decision with its why, and anything meant to change a customer's behaviour is tracked as a bet, with a before number and an after number.

The bets keep me honest. Before Payment Validator is allowed to reject invoices on its own, the customer and I agree the highest false-rejection rate we'll accept. In watch-only mode the AI was wrong on about 1% of invoices. If live rejections go above the agreed line, the system drops back to watch-only. The line is set before the switch, not after.
