Meter data a bureau can bill on

Siemens runs an energy bureau. It collects readings from thousands of electricity, gas and water meters and sends each client reports on what they use and what it costs. They were moving these clients off an old platform onto Facilio. The move was late, promised dates kept slipping, and the data coming in was messy. Siemens asked for a product manager to own it. I took it on.

After

Old platform switched off on 10 Sep 2024. Every reading checked by rules. Reports sent on schedule.

What I worked on

Bad readings were reaching client reports.

Meters send spikes, negative values, duplicates and resets. One bad reading makes a whole month's report wrong.

I built System Integrity Manager. It checks every reading against rules, fixes what it can, and alerts the team about what it can't. Live on Siemens meters by Aug 2024, over 1,000 fixes a week. Later released to every Facilio customer.

Missing readings broke the monthly totals.

When a meter goes quiet for a few hours, the gap shows up as zero use and then a big spike.

I built gap filling that spreads the missing usage across the gap the way that meter normally uses energy at that time of day, so the totals still match the meter.

Nobody knew when a meter stopped sending data.

The team found out weeks later, when a client's report came out empty.

I built meter offline alarms. Each meter shows as connected, partly connected or offline, and Siemens and their vendors get an alert when one goes quiet.

Live problems were found too late.

Readings every half hour are fine for bills, but too slow to catch a fault while it is happening.

I built Live Monitor Readings. Chosen points send readings every 1, 2 or 5 minutes, and an alarm fires the moment a limit is crossed.

Seeing a problem didn't mean you could act on it.

Siemens sells sensors and controls. Operators could see a fault on the dashboard but had to go to another system to do anything about it.

I built control actions. Operators send a command straight from the dashboard, or schedule it around the building's calendar.

Energy projects had to prove their savings.

When Siemens installs an energy-saving project, the client wants proof it worked.

I built savings tracking. It predicts what the site would have used without the project, based on past usage and weather, and shows the real savings in energy, cost and carbon, month by month.

Budgets were guesses.

Clients wanted to know what next year's energy would cost.

I built weather-based forecasting of energy use and budget.

Clients wanted their data in their own format, on time.

The team built every export by hand, and the old tool stopped at 5,000 rows.

I turned the Data Loader into Data Manager: one app to import, fix, re-run and export data. Exports now go up to 10 million rows, use saved templates and run on a schedule. When data is fixed, every total that depends on it re-calculates, so reports are never stale.

What changed

  • 10 Sep 2024: Siemens switched off the old platform.
  • Siemens moved from weekly calls to every two weeks, raised a new contract and planned to bring in about 16,000 more meters.
  • By Jun 2026: 44,000+ meters, 41,000+ sites and 866 clients run through it. About 40,000 data-correction runs a month, about 6,000 scheduled exports a week.
  • One data-quality engine now powers correction, live alarms and export. I built it once instead of three times.
