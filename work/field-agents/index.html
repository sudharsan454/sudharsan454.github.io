Putting the person who did the job back on the record

Technicians and site supervisors don't log into a portal. They live on WhatsApp. So the record of a visit, the attendance, the checklist, the photos, the certificate, gets typed in later by someone in the office who wasn't there.

Where it runs

Before building Bruce, the technician agent for Q3 Services in the UK, I read a year of their own records to see how big the gap really was. For CIT Group in Saudi Arabia, I built a supervisor agent so site supervisors raise jobs and add after-photos from WhatsApp.

The head asked for one thing. The ground said another.

A CIT head wanted supervisors to create work orders in WhatsApp, nothing more. I built that, and the needle didn't move. Then I spoke to the supervisors. Jobs were still being closed by the helpdesk team in the Facilio app, as extra work on top of their own. So I built the whole loop in a few days: create, start, complete, all in WhatsApp. Within weeks the supervisors' share went from 1.2% to 7.9%. I showed the head the numbers, and he agreed the full loop was the better answer.

What the records showed

  • The role for site operatives existed in the system, with nobody assigned to it.
  • Only about a quarter of jobs were closed off on the day. A third were still changing a month later.
  • 62% of on-demand jobs had no request behind them, because phone and email requests never became records.

That gave us a baseline to measure against, set before the agent went live. Targets were left for the customer to set.

What the agents do

The technician agent walks the job as the technician lives it: accept or reject, attendance, insurance and water-risk questions, checklist and meter readings, further works, the compliance date, the service certificate filed straight from a WhatsApp photo, then resolve.

The supervisor agent lets supervisors raise a work order from the field and attach after-photos as proof of completion.

Watch for in the recordings: the form opens inside WhatsApp; after saving, "Create another work order" or close and you're back in the chat. In Bruce, Next jumps straight to the next reading, nothing is saved without a confirm step, and "Back to chat with Bruce" returns the technician to the conversation. Taps at real speed, waiting cut, typing sped up. Test work orders; staff names blurred.

A rule I hold to

The state rules live in the platform, not the prompt. Seven functions check the record before every move and refuse illegal ones. A job can't be resolved without its checklist, insurance answers and, on planned work, its compliance date.

How I build an agent

I start from the flow, the people doing it today and the baseline numbers. The steps are fixed; the model handles the language. I choose the model, write the prompt, define the functions it can call and decide when each one is called.

What I'd do differently

Talk to the people on the ground before the first build, not after. Asking only the head of the account cost us a round.
