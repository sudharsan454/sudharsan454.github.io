Every field was there. Nothing said what to do next.

A US cleaning contractor was live on our field service product and unhappy. The team had built everything the customer asked for, and the customer still had too many steps, too many clicks and cases that slipped through. The customer asked for me to join. I spent a month inside their team and rebuilt four record types around two questions: what is missing, and what do I click next.

What I walked into

The product had every field the customer had asked for. What it did not have was an answer to the questions their team asked a hundred times a day: can we use this vendor yet, which services still have no price, has anyone asked the vendors, will this contract actually bill. Each answer was spread over three or four tabs, or lived in someone's head. Approvals happened in three different modules. Records went out half done because nothing stopped them.

Role
Product lead, embedded with the customer's team for a month, at their request
Built
A Command Centre home page, and a stepper plus a next-step line on vendors, proposals, RFQs and contracts
AI used
None. The problem was screens that said nothing.

The question I asked for a month

Not "what is missing from the product". That question gets you more fields. I asked, for every record, "what do you need to know the moment you open this?" Every answer was one of three things: where is it in the process, what is blocking it, and what do I click next. So that became the rule for every screen: say where the record is, say what is missing in red, and name the one next action using the exact words on the button.

Before and after, module by module

The "before" screens are the stock summary pages from the customer's own org, captured on test records. The "after" screens are the live product. Click any screen to see it full size.

Vendor onboarding

Can we use this vendor yet?

Before
To know if a vendor was ready you opened Insurance, W9 and Supplier Diversity one by one and kept the checklist in your head. The summary itself answered nothing.
After
The record says it in one line. Missing items are red, the stepper shows where onboarding stands, and the Tab key jumps between the fields still to fill.

Client proposal

Which services still have no price?

Before
The summary told you who created the record and when. The real work, which service still needed a vendor and which needed a client rate, sat on a separate tab with no flag.
After
A progress strip, a blue bar that names the exact button to click, and each pending rate in red. Internal services hide the vendor columns, so the page only asks for what applies.

Request for quotation

Has anyone asked the vendors yet?

Before
To check an RFQ you left the proposal, opened another app and read a page laid out like a letter. Nothing said whether vendors had been added or whether quotes were in.
After
RFQs sit on the proposal itself. The stepper reads vendors added, published, quotes in, shortlisted, awarded. A missing spend limit is called out before a quote can be checked against it.

Client contract

Will this contract actually bill?

Before
A contract could be published with no billing cycle and no credit note rule. In this business that means no invoices are raised and no credits for missed visits, and nobody is told.
After
Publish is blocked until the billing setup and every service line are complete. The panel names each gap and what it costs: no billing cycle, no invoices.

Command Centre

What is waiting on us today?

Before

Nothing. The home page was a list of modules. To find out what needed action you opened each one and scrolled.

Before
There was no single answer to "what is waiting on us". Proposals, RFQs, contracts, vendors and approvals each had their own list, and the reason a record was stuck was not on any of them.
After
One page, read live. Each card shows the count and why: rates missing, no quotes received, billing incomplete, insurance expired. Click a card, then a row, and you are on the record with the next step already named.

What it changed for the team

  • Approvals in one place. Invoices, credit notes and netted finance each had their own approval screen. Now only netted finance does.
  • Contracts can't go out half done. A contract with no billing cycle or no credit note rule cannot be published. Before, it could, and it would raise no invoices.
  • No more tab hunting. Onboarding status, pending rates, RFQ stage and contract gaps are on the record's first screen, not three tabs deep.
  • One page for the day. The Command Centre replaces opening each module to find out what is waiting.

How I tested it

Contract changes in this business create invoices and credit notes, so a mistake costs real money. I ran about 250 test cases through the full life of each change and checked every record it created, not the screen. Early runs flagged problems that weren't real because the tests were missing context. Once that was fixed they found 7 to 8 real bugs, and all of them were sorted before the customer saw the change.

The number I don't have

How long it took to onboard a vendor, or to get a proposal priced, before I started. I did not capture a baseline on day one, so the proof on this page is structural: what the screen could not tell you then, and what it tells you now. Every project since starts with that baseline in the kickoff.
