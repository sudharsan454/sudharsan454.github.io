1 · Summary

Short on time? Read this summary, then go to the three decisions. The proof for every point is in section 3 and the screenshots.

The short version

  • Animaker is well liked: 4.7 stars from 1,075 reviews on G2. Its character library is a real strength that no competitor matches.
  • But a free visitor cannot finish making a video. On one AI path, I could not even watch the video I made. It asked me to upgrade first.
  • On the other path I could watch it, but not download it: "Your current plan does not support downloads." A free user can download zero videos.
  • The AI also makes a video only once. It cannot change its own work. Every change has to be done by hand in the editor.
  • Adoption needs two things: make a video and download it. Our own product rules block both.
  • The fix is three decisions, not a rebuild. Our own Steve AI already lets free users watch and download, with a watermark. That shows the approach works.
  • My estimate: these three decisions take adoption from 25 to 50 out of every 100 visitors, with no new traffic.

5 · The three decisions

Three decisions for AI Animation 2.0

Decision 1 · Where we charge

Let everyone watch and download with a watermark. Charge for the clean video.

  • Today we ask for money before showing the result. Leading AI video products show the result first and charge at download. Our own Steve AI does the same.
  • By the time the video exists, we have already paid to make it. Hiding it saves nothing. It only loses the user's trust at the moment we should be proving our value.
  • New rule: every visitor can type an idea, watch the video with a watermark and download it with a watermark, for free. Paying removes the watermark and unlocks HD and more videos. The same rule on every path.
Proof: Steve AI works this way today seen by me; Canva, Synthesia, HeyGen and Fliki all charge at download or for higher quality, never for watching other productsGain: about 10 more users per 100 visitors
Decision 2 · One way in

One starting point. One login. Don't make users pick the technology.

  • Today a new user sees four names: Max, "Animaker Video", "Gen AI video" and Steve. There are two logins, and opening one product logs you out of the other.
  • I have more than 8 years as a product manager, and I could not tell which product is "AI Animation".
  • So I asked support in writing. The answer made it more confusing. I was told to leave Steve and use Max to change my existing video with a prompt. Max cannot do that: it has no video upload and no prompt editing. If our own support team can't point a user to the right product, our product names won't either. (Support email, 21 Sep 2026.)
  • In 2.0 there is one prompt box. Max reads what the user wants and picks the right AI behind the scenes. The user can change the style after seeing the first draft. One login works across Animaker and Steve.
  • Which AI model runs underneath is our problem, never the user's.
Proof: the choose-the-technology screen, the logout, the Max and Steve confusion (screenshots 2 and 7) seen by meGain: about 4 more users per 100 visitors
Decision 3 · The big product change

Let the AI edit its own video: an "Edit with AI" option on every video, scene by scene.

  • Today the AI makes the video once and stops. All three paths work this way. There is no way to say "fix scene 2".
  • On the Gen AI path it gets worse: "Advanced Edit" opens an editor full of 2D cartoon characters for a video made of realistic footage. They don't match.
  • In 2.0, every video has two buttons: Edit manually and Edit with AI. Each scene can be remade from a written instruction. The manual editor stays for what it does well: text, timing, audio and branding.
  • This also protects us from competitors. General AI video tools can't let a user change one scene while keeping consistent, brand-safe characters. We can, because we own the character library.
  • Support already promises this. Asked in writing what Max is for, Animaker support replied "describe the changes you want and continue editing the video". The product can't do that today. 2.0 delivers what support already promises. (Support email, 21 Sep 2026.)
Proof: the AI makes the video once on all 3 paths seen by me; reviewers call the AI "a draft tool, you finish by hand" reviews / companyGain: about 8 more users per 100 visitors

Bugs to fix right away seen by me

These are bugs, not strategy. None of them needs a decision to start. How long they take is for the engineering team to estimate. I am only putting them in order.

  • When Generate fails: show a retry button, keep the typed idea and show a plain error message
  • Add "back to dashboard" and "delete" buttons inside the editor
  • Label the attach button on the prompt box
  • Show one clear message at every paywall: "You can't download because X. Fixing it costs Y." Put all other limits on one plan page
  • Add clickable sample ideas to Max's prompt box. Steve already has them

Together these add about 3 users per 100. Most of that comes from not losing people to errors that today look like "user lost interest".

6 · Competition

Why this matters now: the competition

Looked at through Porter's five forces, the standard way of mapping competitive pressure:

EXISTING COMPETITORS · HIGH

Three groups of competitors

Vyond and Powtoon compete with us directly. Synthesia and HeyGen are winning customers who make training videos. Canva includes AI video in a product people already pay for. The competition is now about who gets a user to their first video fastest.

NEW STARTUPS · HIGH

New startups built on rented AI

New AI models make video tools cheap to build. Each new startup goes after one use case. Storylane (YC) took product demos. Trainn took training videos. Both use cases are on our own homepage.

ALTERNATIVES · HIGH

People need understanding, not just a video

What people really want is "help someone understand X". A video is only one way to do that. Interactive demos and AI avatar clips also do it. So do AI assistants: Claude or ChatGPT can make an animated web page that feels close to a video, can be changed with a prompt, and is free. For training and internal explainers, that can replace the video file. It can't replace video where a file is required, such as YouTube, ads, LMS uploads and WhatsApp. Midjourney also changed what users expect: see the result instantly, then improve it. Users bring both expectations to us.

CUSTOMERS · MEDIUM TO HIGH

Free users can switch at no cost

People on free plans compare several tools in one sitting. They only stay once they have downloaded a video and saved their work. Our paywall blocks exactly that moment.

AI MODEL PROVIDERS · MEDIUM

AI providers control our costs

The Gen AI option uses AI models from other companies. Those companies set our costs and could compete with us directly. The character library is the one thing we fully own, and Decision 3 is built on it.

What this means: we can't win on AI quality or price alone. Our advantage is editable, brand-safe characters: the library, the editor and scene-level control together. That advantage only lasts if users keep their work with us, through saved projects, brand kits and downloaded videos. Today most users leave before any of that happens.

7 · How to check

How we check this works

  • First, track every step. Record each step of the journey: idea submitted, generate failed (kept separate from the user leaving, because today a broken button and a bored user look the same in the data), video watched, editor opened, download clicked, download finished, and paywall shown along with the reason.
  • Then, measure today's numbers. Let the current product run unchanged until the numbers are steady. This replaces each "my estimate" label in section 5 with a real number.
  • Then, test the paywall change. Give half of the free users Decision 1 (free watermarked watching and download). Watch three numbers: downloads, upgrades, and the AI cost per user who downloads. If downloads go up and payments hold, roll it out to everyone and fund Decision 3. If not, all we spent was the cost of some watermarked videos.

On conversion, which is outside this brief: free watermarked downloads give us free marketing from people who don't pay, and the moment someone needs a clean video becomes the natural moment to pay. The same change helps move conversion from 1% to 2%.

8 · Open questions

Three questions for the team

  • Which advantage will be hardest for competitors to copy over the next two years? The character library, the 35 million users, or the patents? From the outside, I think it is the library together with the editor. This plan aims to make users stay because of it, before general AI tools catch up.
  • Is the credit count accurate? On my first try I was told my credits were used up before I saw anything. The same account made another video later that day. The answer tells us if blocking the video was a pricing choice or a billing bug. Those need very different fixes.
  • What does the free plan include, in one sentence? I could not find that sentence anywhere in the product, and I was looking for it as a buyer. If we can't write it down, users can't trust it.
